# 1. Position the Offer

[Back to Capability Map](concept-map)

**The situation:** Someone asks "so what do you do?" and you give a fifteen-second answer that names technologies (FileMaker, AWS, AI integrations) instead of outcomes. Prospects nod politely and file you under "generic technical consultant." You know your work saves clients thousands of hours a year, but the words coming out don't transmit that.

**What changes:** You learn to lead with who the offer is for and what measurably changes for them, not with what you're good at. You can state a positioning sentence — "I help [who] achieve [measurable outcome] via [differentiated mechanism] without [the pain of the status quo]" — and every downstream artifact (outbound messages, discovery questions, proposals) inherits its language from it. You also learn what you're *not*: positioning is as much about repelling bad-fit clients as attracting good ones.

**You're ready when:** You can deliver a 30-second positioning statement that a non-technical business owner could repeat to their spouse. A stranger in your target market hears it and asks a follow-up question instead of nodding. You can name the two or three alternatives your prospects consider (including "do nothing" and "hire a generalist") and why you win against each.

### Practice This

Write your positioning statement using Dunford's components: competitive alternatives, unique capabilities, value, target-market characteristics. Then say it out loud to someone who is *not* in tech. If they can repeat the gist back, keep it. If they say "so... IT stuff?", revise.
