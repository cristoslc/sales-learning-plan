# 3. Run Discovery that Surfaces Pain

[Back to Capability Map](concept-map)

**The situation:** A prospect says "we need help modernizing our systems" and you spend the next 45 minutes explaining what you could do. You leave the call having talked for 80% of it, with no idea what the problem actually costs them, who else feels it, or whether they'll spend money to fix it. You write a proposal anyway.

**What changes:** You learn discovery as structured diagnosis: ask Situation and Problem questions briefly, then spend your time on Implication questions — what does this cost, what happens if it isn't fixed, who else is affected? Top performers ask significantly more implication-type questions than average performers [1][2]. You also learn to talk less: a discovery call where the prospect does most of the talking is the goal, and the ratio is observable in any call recording.

**You're ready when:** You can run a 30-minute discovery where you speak less than 40% of the time and leave with: the problem in the prospect's own words, a rough cost of inaction, who owns it, and what they've already tried. You have a written question bank you actually use.

### Start here

| Resource | Format | Time | Why this one |
|----------|--------|------|-------------|
| *SPIN Selling* — Neil Rackham | Book | ~6 hrs | The research-backed questioning methodology, based on analysis of 35,000+ sales calls. The Situation→Problem→Implication→Need-payoff spine is the backbone of discovery that doesn't feel like an audit [6]. |
| [30 Minutes to President's Club — discovery episodes](https://www.30mpc.com/) | Podcast | 30 min/ep | Word-for-word question phrasings from top sellers. Faster to absorb than the book; use together. |

### Go deeper

| Resource | Format | Time | What it adds |
|----------|--------|------|-------------|
| *Gap Selling* — Keenan | Book | ~5 hrs | Reframes the whole sale around the gap between current and future state; strong on why "no decision" happens. |
| *The Challenger Sale* — Dixon & Adamson | Book | ~6 hrs | Adds teaching to questioning: bring a hypothesis about their business, use discovery to test it rather than to survey. |
| [Gong Labs](https://www.gong.io/labs/) | Data essays | 10 min each | Evidence on question counts, talk ratios, and what distinguishes winning discovery calls. |

### Practice This

Before your next discovery call, write out your Implication and Need-payoff questions in advance — don't wing it [1]. After the call, score yourself: How many implication questions did you ask? What's the cost of the status quo in the prospect's words? Could you restate their problem without using your own vocabulary? Revise the question bank after every call.
