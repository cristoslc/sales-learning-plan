# 4. Quantify the Pain

[Back to Capability Map](concept-map)

**The situation:** Your prospect agrees the problem is real. "It's a real time-suck," they say. Nobody has a number. Two months later the project stalls because the status quo — annoying but survivable — beat your proposal in the budget meeting. You had a problem story; you didn't have a business case.

**What changes:** You learn to convert pain into numbers the buyer already uses to make decisions: hours, error rates, revenue leakage, risk exposure. You learn to walk the implication chain out loud — "if that's 4 hours a week per person across 12 people, that's ~2,500 hours a year; what's a loaded hour worth here?" — and to co-build the number with the prospect so it's *their* number, not yours. This is the "Implicate the Pain" muscle in MEDDPICC and the source of every credible ROI case.

**You're ready when:** For any active opportunity, you can state the cost of doing nothing in the prospect's own numbers, and the prospect has said the number out loud before you did. You can distinguish a nice-to-have from a funded priority by whether a metric is attached.

### Practice This

Take your last three proposals. For each, write: the quantified cost of the status quo (or "not quantified"). For the next live opportunity, co-build one number with the prospect using their inputs. Put the number and its assumptions in the proposal.
