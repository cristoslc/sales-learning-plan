# 5. Find the Real Buyer and Build a Champion

[Back to Capability Map](concept-map)

**The situation:** You've had three great calls. Everyone you've talked to likes you. Then silence. What you didn't know: your contact can't approve a $60K engagement, the CFO has a competing initiative, and nobody inside the company is actively selling your work when you're not in the room. A friendly contact is not a champion, and an enthusiast is not an economic buyer.

**What changes:** You learn to map the buying unit early: who feels the pain, who owns the budget, who can veto, and who will advocate. You learn the test for a real champion — they *sell when you're absent*, give you access to power, and share internal information a polite contact won't [2]. You learn to convert a friendly contact into a champion (co-build the business case, arm them with a one-pager for their boss) or to recognize within two calls that you don't have one.

**You're ready when:** For every active deal you can answer: who signs, who approves budget, who can kill it, and who is actively selling for you internally — with evidence, not hope. In owner-operated businesses you'll often find the champion and economic buyer are the same person, which shortens everything; your discipline is detecting that *fast* in multi-stakeholder situations.

### Practice This

For your most active opportunity, draw the map: Economic Buyer / Champion / Influencers / Veto-holders, with one piece of evidence per box. Identify the weakest box and design your next meeting to strengthen it (usually: ask your champion for a joint meeting with their boss).
