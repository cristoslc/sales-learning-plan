# 6. Qualify with MEDDPICC

[Back to Capability Map](concept-map)

**The situation:** It's the end of the month (or quarter, or your runway). You have four "active" opportunities. You tell yourself (or your family, or your calendar) that two will close. Honestly, you don't know — because "they liked the demo" is not evidence, and you never wrote down what was missing. Deals you counted on slip; deals you wrote off close.

**What changes:** You learn a qualification framework as a *diagnostic*, not a CRM checklist: after every meaningful interaction, score each MEDDPICC element green/yellow/red, and let the reds tell you the next best action [3]. MEDDPICC = Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Paper Process, Implicate the Pain, Champion, Competition. It originated at PTC in the 1990s and evolved through MEDDIC → MEDDICC → MEDDPICC for modern cloud-procurement realities [3][4]. Two disciplines matter most: qualifying *out* (the framework is as much for declining bad-fit work as pursuing good work), and treating it as dynamic — each letter evolves as the buyer's reality changes, so you update it continuously rather than filling it in once [3].

**You're ready when:** You can produce a one-page MEDDPICC scorecard for any opportunity, with evidence (a name, a number, a date) behind each letter — and you've killed at least one deal on paper for missing elements. You commit only deals with buyer evidence across the letters and move deals back when evidence weakens [3].

**Adapting it down:** MEDDPICC was built for $100K+, 6+ stakeholder, 90-day+ enterprise deals [4]. For a solo consultancy selling $10–60K engagements to owner-operators, MEDDIC alone is usually enough — Paper Process is often trivial and Competition is often just "do nothing" or "hire a generalist." The skill is knowing which letters are load-bearing in *your* deals and ruthlessly checking those.

### Practice This

Build a personal one-page MEDDPICC scorecard (8 rows, evidence column, red/yellow/green). Backfill it for your three most active opportunities. Be honest: "I think the CFO would approve" is red, not green, without evidence. Pick the reddest element and design your next conversation to turn it yellow. Kill anything with 4+ reds.
