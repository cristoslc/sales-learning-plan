# 7. Write Proposals that Close (and Survive the Paper Process)

[Back to Capability Map](concept-map)

**The situation:** You send a proposal after a good call. Then: silence for two weeks, "can you send it as a PDF," "our accountant needs to look at it," "legal wants changes to the MSA." The deal dies not from disagreement but from friction. On your side, the proposal is a pricing document; on their side, it's the artifact that has to justify the spend to someone who wasn't on the call.

**What changes:** You learn that the proposal is a sales document, not an invoice: it restates the problem in the buyer's words, quantifies the cost of the status quo (Capability 4), names the alternatives considered, and presents your approach as the logical choice. You learn the structure that lets a non-technical approver say yes without you in the room — which is the whole job, since the champion has to re-sell it internally. You also learn the mundane machinery: what signature authority looks like in your target clients, MSA/SOW structure, deposits and payment terms, and how to set a mutual action plan with dates so "paper process" doesn't become a black hole.

**You're ready when:** Every proposal you send has: problem restatement, quantified pain, options (not one price), a clear "what happens next" section, and a follow-up date already agreed before you sent it. You know, before proposing, whether your contact can actually sign at that amount.

### Practice This

Rewrite your standard proposal template: page 1 is the problem and its cost in their numbers; the pricing is optioned (good/better/best); the last page is a mutual action plan with dates. For the next proposal, agree the review date and decision-maker *before* sending.
