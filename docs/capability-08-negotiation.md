# 8. Negotiate without Discounting

[Back to Capability Map](concept-map)

**The situation:** "This is more than we budgeted." You panic, discount 20%, and the deal closes at a margin that makes the work feel heavy. Worse: the discount taught the client that your first price was fiction, and the next engagement starts from the discounted anchor. For a solo consultant, every point of discount is pure margin — there's no sales team to absorb it.

**What changes:** You learn negotiation as a conversation about *value and scope*, not a haggle over price. You learn to respond to price pressure by reducing scope or trading (payment terms, start date, a case study) rather than cutting rate; to stay silent after naming a number; to ask calibrated questions ("what would need to be true for this to work?") instead of defending the price; and to recognize when "we can't afford it" really means "I'm not convinced of the value yet" — which sends you back to Capability 4, not to your rate card.

**You're ready when:** You have a written fallback ladder (what you trade before you ever touch price) and you've held your rate at least once under direct pressure. You can distinguish budget objection (fixable via scope/timing) from value objection (back to discovery) from polite no (move on).

### Start here

### Practice This

Write your trade ladder for the next pricing conversation: 3 things you can give (payment terms, scope reduction, pilot pricing for a *smaller* first phase) and your walk-away. Practice saying your number, then closing your mouth — literally rehearse the pause. Next discount request: respond with a scope trade, not a rate cut.
