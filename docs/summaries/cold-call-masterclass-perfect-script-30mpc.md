## Major Topics Discussed

### The full script: opener → pitch → objection handling

The episode breaks down the complete cold-call script from start to finish — the opener, the pitch, and how to handle the objections that come off it — with real examples and "zero theory fluff." It's the same framework the hosts have used as individual contributors, operators, and leaders.

### The "Heard the name tossed around" opener

A specific opener pattern that frames the call as though the prospect's name had come up naturally — effectively starting from a mini-introduction rather than a cold hit, which breaks the telemarketer stigma that usually causes an instant hang-up.

### Context-first openers vs. permission-begging

The core principle: open with context and relevance ("here's why I'm calling, here's what I know about you/your business") instead of begging for permission ("do you have 30 seconds?"). Context-first signals you did your homework and command a more respectful conversation.

### Pitch by leading with the problem (not the product)

The pitch positions around the buyer's problem first, then presents the solution — selling the situation and its pain before ever talking features. This keeps the buyer engaged and avoids the reflex "we're already covered / send me an email."

### Handling objections the "Mr. Miyagi way"

Objections are handled by deflecting and redirecting (the "Mr. Miyagi method") rather than pushing back hard — so you stay composed and non-pushy instead of sounding desperate, which preserves the relationship and keeps advancing the call.

### Live calls: where reps get stuck, and tone/EQ

After the script walkthrough, the hosts make real cold calls on camera. These prove the script in live conversations and show exactly where reps tend to get stuck, and how tone, confidence, and EQ make or break delivery.
