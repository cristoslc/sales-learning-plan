# KEY TAKEAWAYS: MEDDICC VALUE

- **Author:** MEDDICC (published by MEDDICC; webinar by CEO Andy Whyte)
- **Published:** date not available from source (webinar held Thursday 21st November)
- **Site:** MEDDICC

## Key Takeaways

MEDDICC Value is a value framework built on MEDDPICC that gives the whole GTM team — Sales, Marketing, Customer Success — a single common language for talking about value across the full customer lifecycle, from before the first meeting through delivery. Its core model distinguishes three ways to engage customers — product selling, ROI selling, and value selling — and argues value selling (customer-focused and consultative, aimed at the Economic Buyer) is the best because it positions everything around how your solution helps the customer and sets achievement goals together. It reframes value as a set of M1 → M2 → M3 value stories, which together create a handover loop where M2s become M3s, and achieved M3s become M1s again.

> 1. **GTM is broken by fragmented language** — each function talks about value, stakeholders, and process differently, so there's no united way to position, sell, or deliver value.
> 2. **Three ways to sell against the same pain** — product selling (competition-focused, for the *user*), ROI selling (return-led, for the *owner*), and value selling (customer-focused, consultative, for the Economic Buyer). ROI selling is "good, but you can do better."
> 3. **Why value selling is hard** — skill gap, fear of change, lacking confidence, short-term focus, no GTM support/data, and no value framework to talk about it.
> 4. **The value story ladder — M1, M2, M3** — M1 is a use-case story of delivered value; M2 tailsors that value to a specific customer (solving the "discovery paradox"); M3 is a shared value goal you achieve with the customer.
> 5. **M2s solve the discovery paradox** — prospects want to know what you can do for them without answering anything; customers hate blind pitching and interrogation but love research-driven relevance and reference selling through conversation.

## Main Arguments & Points

- **Go-to-market is broken by siloed language.** Sales, Marketing, and Customer Success each have their own way of talking about value, stakeholders, and process, so teams can't work together cohesively. Without a common language, there's no united way to position, sell, or deliver value.
- **MEDDPICC is the natural common language.** MEDDICC Value builds on existing processes and uses MEDDPICC as the universal GTM vocabulary because it meets three criteria: it must be *common*, work for *every GTM team*, and work for the *entire customer lifecycle*.
- **Value selling is one of three engagement modes — and the best one.**
  - *Product selling* suits the users; it's competition-focused, talks features and benefits, and the most selling it does is a demo.
  - *ROI selling* suits the owner; performance-focused, frequently mistaken for value selling, positioned with an ROI calculator and an ROI business case. "It's good, but you can do better."
  - *Value selling* suits the Economic Buyer (and honestly the owner too); it's customer-focused, consultative, and the hardest to do — but the best, because it positions everything around how the solution helps customers, and delivery is about setting goals to achieve together.
- **Value selling attaches to the three kinds of pain** — economic, efficiency, and risk-related. Different solutions may solve the same pain, but value selling is where you make a real difference and cinch the deal.
- **Why value selling is hard** — a skill gap (you must know the product and industry inside and out); fear of change (successful teams don't see a reason to change until it's too late); lacking confidence (if you don't believe you can sell this way, you won't); short-term focus (rushing a deal runs into trouble when value isn't realized); no GTM support (no data to talk about the value you provide); and no value framework (no way of talking about it).
- **The M1 / M2 / M3 value stories.** MEDDICC Value keeps the customer at the center of the GTM Triangle, surrounding them with value as they move from M1 to M2 to M3:
  - An **M1** is a value story based on a use case with a previous customer — the PAIN without your solution, the GAIN with your solution, the METRICS used to measure the delta, the DECISION CRITERIA required to replicate it, and the STAKEHOLDERS involved.
  - An **M2** is a personalized value story about *your* customer, tailoring the hypothesized value of the M1 specifically to them.
  - An **M3** is a value goal focused on *delivering* value — proposing value and setting it as something you and the customer work toward achieving together.
- **M1/M2/M3 are use-case based** — one customer may have delivered multiple kinds of value, but each story addresses one specific element.
- **M1s benefit the whole GTM team** — built with MEDDIC, an M1 explains how your solution delivered value to a customer; SDRs use them to tailor outreach, and product teams use them to understand which features are relevant to customers.
- **M2s solve the discovery paradox** — customers want to know what you can do for them without having to answer any questions. They hate blind pitching, being interrogated, or having assumptions made about them — but they love it when you use research to be relevant, tell them stories, and reference-sell — all through conversation.
- **The handover loop** — MEDDPICC + MEDDICC Value "creates the world's best handover, as M2s become M3s, which, when achieved, become M1s again."

## Notable Details & Examples

- The **GTM Triangle** concept positions value *around* the customer at every point of the M1 → M2 → M3 journey.
- The distinction that **ROI selling is not value selling** is a central clarification: ROI is return-led and often mistaken for value selling, but value selling is broader — customer-focused and consultative, engaging the Economic Buyer.
- The **"discovery paradox"** is named and directly solved by M2s: the customer wants evidence of value without being interrogated, and personalized value stories deliver that.
- The **three pain types value selling attaches to** (economic, efficiency, risk-related) give the framework a concrete target for where it "cinches the deal."
