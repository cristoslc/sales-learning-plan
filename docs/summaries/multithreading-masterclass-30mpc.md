## Major Topics Discussed

### The framing: C-suite thinking vs. feature selling

The masterclass's core premise: "The C-suite doesn't think in features, demos, or pain points — they think in strategic objectives, risk, and resource trade-offs. If your message doesn't map to those priorities, the deal never gets above middle management." That reframing drives everything that follows.

### Executive messaging, discovery, and consensus frameworks

The episode is organized around the frameworks that let top performers close high-value deals:
- **Executive messaging** — from the first email, speak to the buyer's strategic objectives and risks, not to a feature list.
- **Discovery** — surface what matters to each stakeholder and connect it to their incentives.
- **Consensus building** — the big group meeting with power is where deals are won; plan for it explicitly.

### Multi-threading mechanics (the Joint Expansion Plan / JEP)

Practical tactics for building committee coverage:
- **Provide context before asking for access to power** — never cold-ask for an executive intro without a reason tied to their priorities.
- **Start the plan from critical events and work backwards** — the go-live, merit cycle, valuation, or contract date determines the steps and their order.
- **Mitigate risk by establishing and aligning the next three steps of the buying cycle** — so no one is surprised at an 11th-hour decision.
- **Request specific names early** — naming the stakeholders you need makes the ask concrete and easy to grant.

### The mutual action plan (MAP)

- **Identify your champion and the problem you are solving first, then share the MAP** — the plan is an agreement, not an ambush.
- **Map the key milestones and customize them for the people who need to be involved** — procurement, security, finance are all on the plan.
- **Don't reveal every step up front** — show the big stages, then deep-dive the current stage so no one feels overwhelmed.
- **Anchor the MAP to the buyer's go-live date** — it signals you will return to those steps later and keeps the timeline real.
