## Core Thesis

The richest source of new revenue sits inside accounts you already serve, and it is earned by **proving value, not by waiting for a renewal to appear**. Great account managers create expansion pipeline the same way sellers prospect net-new: define the executive outcome, deliver and document the proof, communicate the value back up, earn the right to surface the adjacent problem, and then expand coverage. The discipline is a partnership with customer success, a whitespace map driven by expansion levers, and a closing/review cadence that turns every delivered engagement into renewal and referral material.

## Major Topics Discussed

### Creating expansion pipeline through value

The masterclass's headline reframe: "Treat value delivery like prospecting: identify the executive outcome, deliver the proof, communicate it back, then earn the right to uncover what's next." Expansion is not a product pitch; it is a value story about a delivered outcome that makes the next problem feel obvious and cheap to fix.

### Owning the executive relationship

- Partner with your CSM to keep execution on track while you "stay above the weeds."
- Connect the delivered work to executive priorities so the value is visible to the buyer, not just the user.
- Turn customer success — and the relationship you own — into expansion opportunities, including renewals.

### Mapping whitespace with expansion levers

The account map is driven by expansion levers:
- **Product attachment** — additional modules or seats attached to the existing solution.
- **Scope expansion** — extending the engagement to adjacent problems.
- **Commercial changes** — packaging, term, or pricing changes.
Activate these through **new use cases, modernization, consolidation, and M&A or organizational change** — the trigger events that create whitespace.

### Locking down renewals

Renewals are engineered, not assumed: a closing review that quantifies delivered value, a structured referral ask at the moment of maximum goodwill, and a scheduled re-engagement cadence. The expansion conversation ("now that X works, the adjacent problem Y is much cheaper to fix") is often easier to win than any new client.

### Partnering with CS without stepping on each other's toes

Clear ownership between sales and customer success is the guardrail: sales owns the executive relationship and the expansion motion, CS owns execution — so the two reinforce rather than compete, and renewals stop leaking.
